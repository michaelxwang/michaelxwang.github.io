A Passive Foreign Investment Company (PFIC) defined under [IRC § 1297](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/1297){:target="_blank"} is generally any non-U.S. corporation, mutual fund, or ETF where:

* 75% or more of its gross income comes from passive sources (like interest, dividends, or capital gains), or  
* 50% or more of its assets produce passive income.

If you are a U.S. taxpayer and hold shares in foreign mutual funds, foreign ETFs, or certain foreign corporations (often found in foreign investment accounts), the IRS classifies those investments as PFICs. Check the issuer and fund structure to clarify the reporting requirement.

U.S. shareholders generally file taxes under one of three regimes for each fund: 

* **Qualified Electing Fund (QEF).** Electing under [IRC § 1295](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/1295){:target="_blank"} to treat the PFIC as a QEF allows current taxation of ordinary earnings and net capital gains, building basis and preserving capital gain character upon sale.  
* **Mark-to-Market (MTM).** Available for marketable stock under [IRC § 1296](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/1296){:target="_blank"}; recognizes annual unrealized gains and losses (up to unreversed inclusions) as ordinary income or loss.  
* **Default Section 1291 Rules.** Applies when no election is made under [IRC § 1291](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/1291){:target="_blank"}; taxes excess distributions and disposition gains at the highest marginal rate over the holding period, plus interest charges.

[Form 8621](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8621){:target="_blank"} is the principal reporting form. The following is the summary of reporting rules in each regime.

**QEF**

The fund’s Annual Income Statement (AIS) provides ordinary earnings and net capital gains per share as shown below:

| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| BMO Canadian Bank Income Index ETF | ZBI | 0.0031484545 | 0.0008078236 |

This is the most tax efficient option, and we report as follows:

* We compute the pro-rata share of ordinary earnings and net capital gains based on the weighted average shares held during the fund's fiscal year. Ordinary earnings are reported on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), Line 8z, and net capital gains are reported on Schedule D as capital gain distributions.  
* Dispositions of QEF stock are reported on Schedule D, with gain or loss calculated using the adjusted basis (which is increased by QEF inclusions and decreased by distributions of previously taxed income).  
* Dividends are not reported separately as they are included in PFIC reporting.  
* Both ordinary income and capital gain increase the basis for the final sale of the fund, while distributions of previously taxed income (PTI) decrease the basis.

**MTM**

1. Unrealized gains (comparing the year-end Fair Market Value to the adjusted basis) are reported on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), Line 8z as ordinary income, increasing both adjusted basis and unreversed inclusions.  
2. Unrealized losses are allowed as ordinary losses on Schedule 1, Line 8z only up to the extent of prior unreversed inclusions (previous MTM gains included in income), decreasing adjusted basis and unreversed inclusions.  
3. Any market decline exceeding that amount cannot be taken as a current deduction and is tracked on Form 8621\.  
4. Realized gain on sale of MTM shares is treated as ordinary income.  
5. Realized loss is capped by unreversed inclusions as ordinary loss.   
6. Realized gain on the sale of MTM shares is treated entirely as ordinary income. Realized loss is treated as ordinary loss to the extent of remaining unreversed inclusions, and any excess loss is reported on Schedule D as a capital loss, either short term or long term depending on the hold period.  
7. Dividends are reported normally outside of [Form 8621](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8621){:target="_blank"} as ordinary dividends, which are ineligible for qualified dividend rates under [IRC § 1(h)(11)(C)(iii)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/1){:target="_blank"}.  
8. Taxpayers must separately track stock basis and unreversed inclusions accounts, adjusting for annual MTM gains/losses and prorating accordingly for partial share sales.

**Default Section 1291 Rules**

Here is a step-by-step summary of how the Default Section 1291 Rules ([IRC § 1291](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/1291){:target="_blank"}) work for PFICs:

1. **Trigger Events (Distributions or Sales):** Tax is deferred during ownership; tax events occur upon receiving actual distributions or recognizing gain on sale or disposition.  
2. **Calculate the 125% Threshold:** Compare total distributions in the current year to 125% of the average distributions received over the prior 3 tax years (or the taxpayer's holding period, if shorter).  
3. **Separate Excess vs. Non-Excess Distributions:** Distributions up to 125% are non-excess distributions taxed as ordinary dividends in the current year; amounts exceeding 125% (and all gain on sale) are excess distributions.  
4. **Allocate and Tax Excess Distributions:** Allocate the excess distribution or gain ratably on a daily basis over the taxpayer's entire holding period. The portion allocated to the current year and pre-PFIC years is taxed as ordinary income in the current year. The portion allocated to prior PFIC years is taxed at the highest marginal tax rate for each year, plus an underpayment interest charge from that year's tax return due date.  
5. **Fulfill Reporting via Form 8621:** File [Form 8621](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8621){:target="_blank"} for each PFIC to compute ordinary income, deferred tax, and interest penalties, transferring the deferred tax and interest to Form 1040 as additional taxes.

**Conclusion** 

PFIC taxation rules are highly complex and can result in significant tax liabilities, punitive interest charges, and detailed reporting requirements under [Form 8621](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8621){:target="_blank"}. Selecting the appropriate regime (QEF, MTM, or Section 1291\) depends heavily on individual circumstances, investment structure, and available documentation.
