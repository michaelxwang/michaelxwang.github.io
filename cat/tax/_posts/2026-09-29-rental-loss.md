Rental real estate is treated, by default, as a "passive activity" under the Internal Revenue Code — meaning losses from it can normally only offset passive income, not wages, business profits, or other ordinary income. For most taxpayers, the only relief is a modest \$25,000 allowance that disappears entirely once income rises above \$150,000. This note explains the two statutory paths that remove rental losses from that income-based limitation altogether, and the specific hour and participation requirements each path demands.

**The Default Rule: Rental Losses Are Passive, Regardless of Effort**

[IRC Section 469(c)(2)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"} provides the baseline rule:

*For purposes of this section, the term 'passive activity' includes any rental activity.*

Critically, [Section 469(c)(4)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"} makes clear this classification applies "without regard to whether or not the taxpayer materially participates in the activity." In plain terms, a landlord who personally handles every repair, screens every tenant, and spends thousands of hours a year on a rental property is still, by statute, treated as passive — hours of effort do not by themselves convert a rental into a non-passive activity. Two narrow exceptions change this result, discussed below.

**The Modest Exception Most Taxpayers Already Know: The \$25,000 Allowance**

[IRC Section 469(i)(1)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"} allows a limited exception: taxpayers who "actively participate" in rental real estate may deduct up to \$25,000 of losses against nonpassive income. However, [Section 469(i)(3)(A)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"} phases this allowance out by 50 cents for every dollar of modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) above \$100,000, and it reaches zero at \$150,000 of MAGI. For any taxpayer earning meaningfully more than \$150,000, this allowance provides no benefit whatsoever — regardless of how actively the taxpayer participates.

**Path 1: Real Estate Professional (REP) Status and Material Participation**

**Real Estate Professional (REP) Status**

[IRC Section 469(c)(7)(A)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"} removes the automatic passive classification for a qualifying real estate professional, but only "for purposes of applying this section... rental real estate activities... of a taxpayer shall not be treated as passive activities under paragraph (2) if such taxpayer meets the requirements of subparagraph (B)" of the same section. [Section 469(c)(7)(B)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"} sets out two tests, both of which must be satisfied for the taxable year:

* More-than-half test ([IRC §469(c)(7)(B)(i)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"}): "more than one-half of the personal services performed in trades or businesses by the taxpayer during such taxable year are performed in real property trades or businesses in which the taxpayer materially participates."

* 750-hour test ([IRC §469(c)(7)(B)(ii)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"}): "such taxpayer performs more than 750 hours of services during the taxable year in real property trades or businesses in which the taxpayer materially participates."

[IRC Section 469(c)(7)(C)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"} defines the qualifying activities broadly: "any real property development, redevelopment, construction, reconstruction, acquisition, conversion, rental, operation, management, leasing, or brokerage." This means hours spent as a licensed real estate agent, broker, developer, or property manager all count toward the 750-hour and more-than-half tests — not just hours spent on the taxpayer's own rental property.

**Material Participation**

Meeting these two tests only establishes REP status; it does not, by itself, make any specific rental non-passive. The statute requires an additional, separate step: the taxpayer must materially participate in each specific rental activity under the seven tests set out in [Temp. Treas. Reg. §1.469-5T(a)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/cfr26/1.469-5T){:target="_blank"}:

1. **500-Hour Test (Temp. Treas. Reg. § 1.469-5T(a)(1)):** Participation for more than 500 hours during the year.

2. **Substantially All Test (Temp. Treas. Reg. § 1.469-5T(a)(2)):** Participation that constitutes substantially all of the participation in the activity by all individuals (including non-owners) for the year.

3. **100-Hour/No-More-Than-Others Test (Temp. Treas. Reg. § 1.469-5T(a)(3)):** Participation for more than 100 hours during the year, where no other individual participates more than the taxpayer.

4. **Significant Participation Activity (SPA) Test (Temp. Treas. Reg. § 1.469-5T(a)(4)):** The activity is a significant participation activity (more than 100 hours of participation), and the taxpayer's aggregate participation in all SPAs for the year exceeds 500 hours.

5. **Prior-Year Participation Test (Temp. Treas. Reg. § 1.469-5T(a)(5)):** Material participation in the activity for any five taxable years (whether or not consecutive) during the ten immediately preceding taxable years.

6. **Personal Service Activity (PSA) Test (Temp. Treas. Reg. § 1.469-5T(a)(6)):** The activity is a personal service activity, and the taxpayer materially participated in the activity for any three preceding taxable years (whether or not consecutive).

7. **Facts and Circumstances Test (Temp. Treas. Reg. § 1.469-5T(a)(7)):** Based on all facts and circumstances, the taxpayer participates in the activity on a regular, continuous, and substantial basis during the year (requiring at least 100 hours of participation).

Because each rental property is tested separately by default, a taxpayer with multiple properties may also elect under [Treas. Reg. §1.469-9(g)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/cfr26/1.469-9){:target="_blank"} to aggregate all rental real estate interests into a single activity for purposes of this test.

Once both prongs are satisfied — REP status for the year, and material participation in the specific rental — the loss is fully non-passive. Notably, [Section 469(c)(7)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"} contains no income ceiling of any kind; the benefit is available in full to a taxpayer earning \$50,000 or \$5,000,000.

**Path 2: The Short-Term Rental Exception and Material Participation**

A second, independent path exists entirely outside the real estate professional rules. [Treas. Reg. §1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/cfr26/1.469-1T){:target="_blank"} excludes certain rental arrangements from the definition of "rental activity" in the first place — meaning the automatic-passive rule of Section 469(c)(2) never applies to them at all. Among the listed exclusions is property where "the average period of customer use for such property is seven days or less."

Because such short-term rental activity falls outside the statutory definition of a rental activity, its passive-or-nonpassive status is instead determined under the ordinary material participation rules of [IRC §469(c)(1)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"} and [Temp. Treas. Reg. §1.469-5T(a)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/cfr26/1.469-5T){:target="_blank"} — the same seven tests used for ordinary trades or businesses. A taxpayer who materially participates in a short-term rental (for example, by satisfying the 500-hour test) can deduct losses currently, with no REP status required and no income phase-out of any kind.

**What Happens Without Either Path**

If a taxpayer is not a real estate professional, does not qualify for the short-term rental exception, and earns too much to benefit from the Section 469(i) allowance, the loss is not lost — it is suspended. Under [IRC §469(b)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"}, disallowed passive losses "shall be treated as a deduction or credit allocable to such activity in the next taxable year," carrying forward indefinitely until offset by passive income from any source, or released in full under [Section 469(g)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"} upon a fully taxable disposition of the taxpayer's entire interest in the activity.

**Former passive activity**

Even if you meet the requirements of either path, accumulated losses from prior passive years will not be released immediately. [IRC §469(f)(1)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/usc26/469){:target="_blank"} explicitly provides that "if an activity is a former passive activity for any taxable year, \[it\] shall continue to be treated as arising from a passive activity."

**Proof for Material Participation**

A contemporaneous daily log is not strictly required by law — [Treas. Reg. §1.469-5T(f)(4)](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/cfr26/1.469-5T){:target="_blank"} explicitly provides:

Contemporaneous daily time reports, logs, or similar documents are not required if the extent of such participation may be established by other reasonable means. Reasonable means for purposes of this paragraph may include but are not limited to the identification of services performed over a period of time and the approximate number of hours spent performing such services during such period, based on appointment books, calendars, or narrative summaries.

Therefore, the key is maintaining reliable documentation that can be cross-verified with other supporting facts.
