**FACTS**

Taxpayer incurred currency translation loss which is ordinary loss federally per IRC section 988\.

**ISSUE**

Does Massachusetts allow an individual taxpayer to deduct an [IRC § 988](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/988){:target="_blank"} ordinary loss if it occurred in a personal investment context (i.e., not tied to a Schedule C trade or business)?

**SHORT ANSWER**

No.

**STEP BY STEP ANALYSIS**

1. **Federal character.** [§ 988](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/988){:target="_blank"} makes foreign currency gain or loss ordinary "notwithstanding any other provision," and disposing of nonfunctional currency (including foreign-currency bank deposits) is a § 988 transaction.

2. **Investment versus personal.** § 988(e) takes "personal transactions" out of § 988, but a transaction is not personal to the extent its expenses meet [§ 162](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/162){:target="_blank"} or [§ 212](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/212){:target="_blank"}. An investment-for-profit conversion therefore stays within § 988\.

3. **Federal deduction path.** [Treas. Reg. § 1.988-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.988-2){:target="_blank"} sends the recognition of a currency disposition through the property sale-or-exchange rules. For an individual with no trade or business, the loss is deductible under [§ 165(c)(2)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/165){:target="_blank"}, and it reaches AGI through [§ 62(a)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/62){:target="_blank"}.

4. **Massachusetts starting point.** Under [M.G.L. c. 62, § 2](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleIX/Chapter62/Section2){:target="_blank"}, Massachusetts gross income is federal gross income plus or minus specific modifications. The statute splits income into Part A (interest, dividends, and short-term capital gains), Part B (ordinary income), and Part C (long-term capital gains).

5. **Which part the loss falls in.** §988 loss is ordinary by statute, not capital, so it belongs in Part B. It cannot use the capital-loss rules of Part A or Part C.

6. **Part B deductions.** M.G.L. c. 62, § 2(d)(1) allows Part B deductions allowable under IRC § 62, but explicitly disallows subparagraph (M) ("the deduction allowed by section sixty-two (a)(3) of the Code"). Because § 62(a)(3) is the sole mechanism for a nonbusiness sale-or-exchange loss to reduce gross income, the loss cannot be deducted against Part B income.

7. **Offsetting Part A.** Excess Part B deductions can reduce Part A income only if related to an active trade or business. An individual investor with no trade or business cannot offset Part A income with this loss.
